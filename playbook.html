Data Product Governance Playbook

From Draft to Published — The End-to-End Agentic Governance Lifecycle
📅 May 2026 👤 Sahrear Seraj — Data & AI Architect 📐 ODPS v4.0 🔄 Phase 2 — Active 🌐 waranara.com/agent
Overview

Purpose & Scope

This playbook defines how data products are governed end-to-end within the data mesh programme — from the moment a domain team registers a product in Microsoft Purview, through an AI-assisted gap closure process, to formal publication and continuous quality monitoring.

It covers three interconnected concerns: the data product lifecycle (how a product moves from Draft to Published), the governance standard (ODPS v4.0 field requirements and configurable maturity thresholds), and the Data Product Governance Council (the human oversight body that owns the standard and approves publication).

🎯
Design Principle

Governance in a data mesh must be federated and computational — encoded in automation and tooling, not in meetings and sign-off workflows. The Data Product Intelligence Agent does the work of surfacing and closing governance gaps. The Council owns the standard. Domain teams own their products. No one owns a bottleneck.

The Problem

Why Traditional Data Governance Fails at Scale

⏳
Speed
A data scientist spends 2 days finding out if a dataset is approved for model training. Governance teams are a bottleneck, not an enabler.
📋
Completeness
8 of 12 data products have no assigned DataSteward. 10 of 12 have empty terms of use. Governance gaps are invisible until something goes wrong.
🔄
Sustainability
RACI matrices and approval committees do not scale to a data mesh with multiple domain teams shipping continuously.
⚠️
The Root Cause

Traditional governance treats compliance as a human activity — someone reviews something and approves it. At data mesh scale, that model breaks down. There are too many products, too many domains, and too much change for any committee to keep up.

Vision

Federated Computational Governance

The fourth data mesh principle — Federated Computational Governance — states that governance should be encoded in standards and automation, federated across domain teams, and enforced computationally. The role of a central governance function is to own the standard, not to review every product.

❌ Old Way — Traditional Governance
  • Central committee reviews every data product
  • Data stewards fill in forms manually
  • Compliance checked in annual audits
  • Governance is a bottleneck and a delay
  • Domain teams resent the overhead
✅ New Way — Computational Governance
  • Standards encoded in automation and tooling
  • AI agent surfaces and closes gaps conversationally
  • Compliance checked in every CI/CD pipeline run
  • Governance is invisible — it happens automatically
  • Domain teams self-serve against the standard

Stage 1

Draft — Registration via Purview

A domain team creates a new data product using the PowerApp form connected to Microsoft Purview. At this stage, only a minimal set of fields is required — enough to register the product's existence in the catalogue and governance graph.

💡
Purview → Neo4j Sync

The Purview JSON export is read by the ingestion pipeline and loaded into the Neo4j knowledge graph as a DataProduct node. The governance_config.yaml field mapping translates Purview custom attribute names to ODPS v4.0 field paths. A completeness score is computed on first ingestion. If mandatory fields are missing, the product enters Stage 2 immediately.

Stage 2

Incomplete — The Agentic Gap Closure Loop

This is where the intelligence of the system is most visible. The Data Product Intelligence Agent runs a nightly gap scan against the knowledge graph. For each product below the publication threshold, it identifies the highest-priority missing field and initiates a conversational exchange with the Product Owner via Microsoft Teams or Slack.

How the Agent Prioritises Gaps

The agent reads governance_config.yaml to determine field weights and mandatory status. It always addresses mandatory fields first, in descending weight order. It asks one question per conversation turn — not a list of 12 missing fields.

Design Principle — One Question Per Turn

The agent deliberately asks one question per conversation turn. This matches how Product Owners actually work — they respond to a quick Teams message, not a form with 12 required fields. Validated responses are written back to Neo4j immediately. The agent resumes from where it left off on the next run.

Stage 3

In-Review — Governance Council Notification

When a data product reaches the publication threshold (default: ≥80% completeness + all mandatory fields complete), the agent automatically notifies the Governance Council. The Council reviews and either approves publication or returns the product with specific feedback.

Council Notification — What It Includes

Product Summary
Product name, domain, type, completeness score, mandatory fields status, days in Incomplete stage, Product Owner contact.
Outstanding Optional Gaps
Fields not yet completed that are not mandatory — data access endpoint, DQ rules, use cases. Council decides if these block publication.
DQ Status (if available)
Latest DQ scores from Databricks if pipeline has run. Any active breaches are flagged — Council may defer publication until resolved.
Approval Action
Approve — product moves to Published. Return — Council specifies which fields need resolution before re-submission.
Stage 4

Published — Available for Consumption

A Published data product is discoverable via the Data Product Intelligence Agent at waranara.com/agent, queryable by domain teams, and eligible for AI agent routing decisions (e.g. "which products are permitted for model training?").

🌐
Live Demo

The agent at waranara.com/agent answers governance questions in plain English — no Purview UI, no Cypher knowledge, no ticket to the data team. Type "which data products are permitted for model training?" and the answer comes back in seconds, grounded in the knowledge graph.

Stage 5

Monitored — Continuous Quality Assurance

After publication, Databricks pipelines run DQ checks on each data product's underlying tables. Results are written to a governance Delta table and synced to the Neo4j knowledge graph nightly. The agent monitors for breaches and re-opens a conversation loop with the Product Owner when thresholds are violated.

The DQ Sync Flow

1
Databricks pipeline runs DQ checks

Great Expectations or Databricks Lakehouse Monitoring evaluates rules against actual table data. Example: "Are 99% of first_name values non-null in the customer profile table?"

2
Results written to governance.dq_results Delta table

Each row records product_id, dimension (completeness/uniqueness/etc.), rule_name, threshold, score, passed (true/false), and measured_at timestamp.

3
dq_sync.py reads results and updates Neo4j

DataQualityRule nodes are updated with current scores. Any rule where score < threshold sets dq_breach = true on the node.

4
Agent detects breach and alerts Product Owner

Next morning the agent identifies dq_breach = true nodes and opens a Teams/Slack conversation: "Customer-360 first_name completeness dropped to 80% — below the 99% threshold. Priya, can you investigate?"

Governance Standard

ODPS v4.0 — The Governing Standard

The Open Data Product Specification v4.0 (Linux Foundation) defines the canonical structure for data product metadata. All governance fields in this playbook map to ODPS v4.0 objects. The standard is published at opendataproducts.org/v4.0.

dataQuality (8 dimensions)DataQualityRuleCompleteness, uniqueness, timeliness, validity, accuracy, consistency, accessibility, integrity

Configuration

Configurable Field Weights — governance_config.yaml

The governance standard is configurable without code deployment. The Data Product Governance Lead edits governance_config.yaml in the repository to adjust field weights, mandatory status, and publication thresholds as domain maturity evolves. Changes take effect on the agent's next nightly run.

# governance_config.yaml — Owner: Data Product Governance Lead # Adjust field weights and mandatory status as maturity evolves. # Changes take effect on next agent run — no code deployment required. version: "1.0" publish_threshold: 80 # minimum score to trigger Council review mandatory_block: true # mandatory=true fields block publish regardless fields: - field: data_steward weight: 15 mandatory: true agent_question: "Who on your team is the Data Steward for {product_name}?" - field: data_residency weight: 15 mandatory: true agent_question: "Where must {product_name} data physically reside?" - field: glossary_terms weight: 10 mandatory: false # ← change to true when domain maturity allows agent_question: "What business terms best describe {product_name}?"

Maturity

Data Product Maturity Stages

Rather than a binary published/unpublished state, products progress through maturity stages. This gives domain teams a clear progression and allows products to be useful (internally visible) before they reach the full publication standard.

🥉 Foundation
Score: 0 – 59%
Basic identity and data residency registered. Product exists in the catalogue but is not consumable. Agent is actively closing gaps.
🥈 Governed
Score: 60 – 79%
SLA and licensing defined. Suitable for internal domain consumption — teams within the same domain can use it. Not yet available for cross-domain discovery.
🥇 Published
Score: ≥ 80% + all mandatory fields
Full governance. Council-approved. Available for broad consumption, cross-domain discovery, and AI agent routing decisions.
💎 Certified
Score: ≥ 95%
Gold standard. DQ rules active with live Databricks measurements, data access endpoint configured, CDEs registered on all critical columns. Eligible for AI training and regulatory use cases.
Governance Council

Why a Data Product Governance Council?

The agent enforces the standard computationally. But someone must own the standard itself — deciding which fields are mandatory, resolving cross-domain conflicts, and providing the human judgment that machines cannot. That is the Council's role.

The Council is not a bottleneck. It does not review individual data products in detail — the agent has already validated completeness before any product reaches In-Review. The Council's job is to confirm that a product is ready for publication and to make policy decisions that apply across all domains.

🏛️
Council Mandate

Own the governance standard. Set the rules the agent enforces. Approve products for publication. Escalate domain teams who are not engaging with the curation process. Review and update governance_config.yaml as maturity evolves.

Council — Membership

Council Membership & Operating Cadence

Membership
  • Data Product Governance Lead — Chair
  • Domain Leads × 4 (one per domain)
  • Chief Data Officer or delegate
  • Data Platform Lead

Operating Cadence
  • Monthly meeting — 60 minutes
  • Publication approvals — asynchronous (Teams card) when queue is small
  • Quarterly — review governance_config.yaml threshold adjustments
  • As needed — escalation of stuck products (>30 days in Incomplete)
Council — Agenda

Monthly Council Agenda

📊
1. Maturity Dashboard Review (15 min)
Review completeness scores by domain. Identify products stuck in Incomplete for more than 14 days — escalate to domain lead. Review DQ breach trends from Databricks. Celebrate products that have reached Certified.
✅
2. Publication Approvals (20 min)
Review products in In-Review status. Agent provides a completeness summary card for each. Council votes to Approve or Return with specific feedback. Target: same-week turnaround for straightforward approvals.
📐
3. Standards Update (15 min)
Review proposed changes to governance_config.yaml — new mandatory fields, weight adjustments, threshold changes. Decisions recorded and committed to the governance repo. Agent picks up changes on next nightly run.
🔄
4. Cross-Domain Issues (10 min)
Glossary term conflicts. Shared data asset ownership disputes. Lineage gaps spanning multiple domains. Agent flags these in the graph — Council resolves the policy decision, agent implements the outcome.
Data Quality

DQ Integration — Databricks to Knowledge Graph

Data quality measurement happens in Databricks against actual table data. The governance graph holds the DQ policy (thresholds, dimensions, rules). dq_sync.py bridges the two — reading results from the governance Delta table and updating DataQualityRule nodes in Neo4j nightly.

🔑
Two Separate Concerns — Both Visible in the Agent

Governance completeness (is the data product metadata filled in?) and data quality (are the actual rows in the table valid?) are distinct. The agent surfaces both. A product can have perfect metadata and failing DQ — the agent knows the difference and converses accordingly.

Human Oversight

Human-in-the-Loop Approval Gates

Three gates ensure no metadata is written to the graph, and no product is published, without appropriate human sign-off. The agent cannot override these gates.

Gate 1
Should We Ask?
Trigger: Gap detected in graph

Before the agent sends any message to a Product Owner, the Domain Lead confirms it's appropriate to initiate contact. Prevents agent spam and ensures conversations are expected.

Domain Lead
Manual
Gate 2
Valid Response?
Trigger: Product Owner replies in Teams/Slack

Agent validates response format automatically (e.g. checks email format, validates country code, confirms Party exists in graph). Ambiguous responses escalate to manual review.

Auto + Manual
for ambiguous
Gate 3
Write to Graph?
Trigger: Response validated, ready to write to Neo4j

For ownership and stewardship changes, Domain Lead approves before the graph is updated. For low-risk fields (doc URL, tags), write-back is automatic once validated.

Domain Lead
Manual for ownership
